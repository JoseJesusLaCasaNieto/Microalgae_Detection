### Fluorescence-assisted image analysis of freshwater microalgae
*   **Authors / Source:** Ross F. Walker, Kanako Ishikawa, Michio Kumagai. Journal of Microbiological Methods (2002), 51(2), 149–162.
*   **Key Topics:** Image Analysis, Fluorescence, Automatic Microalgae Classification, Computer Vision, Microscopic Image Processing, Phytoplankton.
*   **Internal PhD Notes:** 
    *   **Source:** Recommended by my PhD supervisors.
    *   **Utility:** An early methodological reference on the detection and automatic classification of microalgae using computer vision techniques. It can serve as a historical antecedent to current AI-based systems for taxonomic identification and automated environmental sample analysis.
*   **Brief Summary:**
    This study develops an automated image analysis system to detect and classify freshwater microalgae using their natural autofluorescence under epifluorescence illumination. Fluorescence enables the localization of microalgae even in complex samples containing sediments, detritus, and other organisms that hinder analysis through conventional microscopy. The authors combine fluorescence and grayscale images, applying image-processing techniques such as morphological segmentation, template matching, and seeded region growing to correct for displacements and deformations of organisms between image acquisitions. Subsequently, they extract 120 morphological and textural features and select an optimal subset for automatic classification using a Bayesian classifier. The system achieved an accuracy greater than 97% in distinguishing between the cyanobacterial genera Anabaena and Microcystis. This study represents a precursor to modern automated microalgae classification approaches, demonstrating how the combination of fluorescence imaging and computational analysis can drastically reduce human intervention in environmental monitoring.

*   **Authors / Source:** Toshiyuki Takahashi. Current Opinion in Green and Sustainable Chemistry (2021), 27, 100410.

*   **Internal PhD Notes:**

    *   **Utility:** Useful review article linking microalgal biorefineries with the need for automated quality-control systems. Particularly relevant for understanding the role of chlorophyll fluorescence and image-based automated cell counters as precursors to modern AI-assisted monitoring and phenotyping platforms for microalgal cultures.
*   **Brief Summary:**
    This review examines the role of microalgal biorefineries as sustainable alternatives to conventional petrochemical processes and emphasizes the importance of reliable biomass monitoring for industrial-scale cultivation. The author compares traditional microalgae evaluation methods, including hemocytometry, turbidity measurements, gravimetry, fluorometry, and flow cytometry, discussing their respective advantages and limitations. Special attention is given to an automated cell counter equipped with chlorophyll fluorescence detection, which combines image-based cell counting with selective identification of living microalgae through chlorophyll autofluorescence. The system enables automatic determination of cell density, discrimination of algae from debris, and assessment of algal physiological status through chlorophyll integrity measurements. According to the reviewed studies, the fluorescence-assisted automated counter achieves cell-counting accuracy comparable to manual hemocytometry while reducing operator bias, measurement time, and sample handling requirements. The article highlights the potential of automated fluorescence-based monitoring systems to support large-scale algal cultivation, quality assurance, and future automation of microalgal biorefineries.

### Microalgae Monitoring in Microscale Photobioreactors via Multivariate Image Analysis
*   **Authors / Source:** Andrea Gozzi, Claudia Cavinato, Francesco Bezzo, Elisa Sforza, Paolo Facco. ChemEngineering (2021), 5(3), 49.
*   **Key Topics:** Microalgae Monitoring, Microscale Photobioreactors (µPBRs), Multivariate Image Analysis, Chlorophyll Fluorescence, RGB Imaging, Biomass Estimation, Chlorophyll-a Prediction, Partial Least Squares (PLS), Process Monitoring.
*   **Internal PhD Notes:** 
    *   **Source:** Recommended by my PhD supervisors.
    *   **Utility:** Highly relevant methodological reference for the use of computer vision and fluorescence measurements as soft sensors in microalgal cultivation systems. Demonstrates how simple RGB images combined with multivariate statistical models can estimate biomass concentration and chlorophyll content without requiring labor-intensive laboratory assays or manual microscopic counting.
*   **Brief Summary:**
    This study proposes a low-cost monitoring strategy for microalgae cultivated in microscale photobioreactors (µPBRs) by combining RGB image analysis, chlorophyll fluorescence measurements, and multivariate regression models. The authors cultivated Scenedesmus obliquus under different light conditions and extracted color features from digital images together with fluorescence indices obtained through PAM fluorometry. These variables were used to build Partial Least Squares (PLS) models capable of predicting biomass concentration and chlorophyll-a content. The best biomass estimation model, combining RGB color indices and fluorescence features, achieved an average relative error of approximately 18.7%, while an enhanced model incorporating measurements from culture dilutions reduced the error to 7.6%. For chlorophyll-a prediction, combining image-derived features with biomass concentration achieved average relative errors close to 13%. The results demonstrate that multivariate image analysis can provide rapid and non-destructive estimates of key cultivation parameters, offering a practical alternative to conventional laboratory measurements. The study highlights the potential of image-based monitoring and fluorescence sensing as foundations for future automated control systems in microalgal bioprocesses and photobioreactor management.

### Perspectives of fluorescence spectroscopy for online monitoring in microalgae industry
*   **Authors / Source:** Mariana Sá, Ivo Teles, René H. Wijffels, Maria J. Barbosa. Microbial Biotechnology (2022), 15(6), 1824–1838.
*   **Key Topics:** Fluorescence Spectroscopy, Online Monitoring, Process Analytical Technology (PAT), Chemometrics, Microalgae Cultivation, Biomass Estimation, Pigment Monitoring, Lipid Quantification, Industrial Photobioreactors.
*   **Internal PhD Notes:** 
    *   **Source:** Recommended by my PhD supervisors.
    *   **Utility:** Comprehensive reference on the use of fluorescence spectroscopy as an online sensing technology for microalgae production. Particularly valuable for understanding how chemometric methods, machine learning approaches, and fluorescence-based sensing can be integrated into industrial monitoring systems to estimate biomass, pigments, lipids, and physiological parameters in real time. It provides a conceptual bridge between traditional fluorescence measurements and modern intelligent monitoring frameworks.
*   **Brief Summary:**
    This review explores the potential of fluorescence spectroscopy as a real-time monitoring technology for industrial microalgae production. The authors describe how excitation–emission matrix (EEM) fluorescence measurements, combined with chemometric techniques such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Partial Least Squares (PLS), and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), can be used to infer critical biological and process variables from complex spectral data. The article reviews applications of fluorescence-based monitoring for cell concentration, chlorophyll content, carotenoids, fucoxanthin, lipid accumulation, fatty acid composition, and biomass quality across multiple microalgal species. Several studies cited in the review demonstrate that fluorescence spectroscopy can accurately estimate biomass concentration over a wide range of cultivation conditions and can even generate predictive models transferable across different species. A central contribution of the paper is its discussion of fluorescence spectroscopy as a Process Analytical Technology (PAT) tool capable of supporting automated decision-making and process control in microalgal biorefineries. The authors argue that fluorescence measurements can provide rapid, non-destructive, and highly sensitive information without extensive sample preparation, enabling continuous monitoring of cultivation and downstream processing operations. They further identify future challenges related to industrial implementation, including sensor miniaturization, simplification of spectrofluorometers, species-independent predictive models, and integration into large-scale photobioreactor systems.

### Recent application of spectroscopy for the detection of microalgae life information: A review
*   **Authors / Source:** Jing-Yan Liu, Li-Hua Zeng, Zhen-Hui Ren. Applied Spectroscopy Reviews, 55(1), 26-59.
*   **Key Topics:** VIS/NIR Spectroscopy, Raman Spectroscopy, FTIR Spectroscopy, Fluorescence Spectroscopy, Chemometrics, Microalgae Life Information (lipids, pigments, biomass).
*   **Internal PhD Notes:** 
    *   **Source:** Recommended by my PhD supervisors.
    *   **Utility:** It will serve as a critical and detailed review of non-destructive optical techniques for bioprocess monitoring within my state of the art or methodological framework.
*   **Brief Summary:**
    This review examines the transition from traditional, destructive chemical methods to innovative, fast, and non-invasive spectroscopic techniques for monitoring the growth status and biochemical composition of microalgae. The paper details the fundamentals and applications of four main technologies: visible and near-infrared (VIS/NIR) spectroscopy, Raman, Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR), and fluorescence spectroscopy. Through chemometric analysis and modeling (such as PLSR, PCA, or LDA), these techniques enable the precise quantification and identification of essential components such as lipid content, pigments (chlorophyll and carotenoids), proteins, carbohydrates, and the discrimination of algae species. Finally, the authors discuss current technical limitations—such as water interference in the infrared or background fluorescence in Raman—and emphasize the need to develop robust processing algorithms and online monitoring models for industrial scale-up.

### Assessment of microalgae species, biomass, and distribution from spectral images using a convolution neural network
*   **Authors / Source:** Pauliina Salmi, Marco Calderini, Salli Pääkkönen, Sami Taipale, Ilkka Pölönen. Journal of Applied Phycology, 34, 1565–1575 (2022).
*   **Key Topics:** Hyperspectral Imaging (HSI), Spectral Absorbance, Convolutional Neural Networks (1D-CNN), Biomass Estimation, Species Classification, Spectral Unmixing, Microalgae Monitoring, Machine Learning.
*   **Internal PhD Notes:** 
    *   **Source:** Recommended by my PhD supervisors.
    *   **Utility:** Particularly useful as a reference for combining hyperspectral imaging with deep learning models for species identification and biomass prediction in mixed cultures. It provides methodological insights that could complement chemometric approaches traditionally used in spectroscopic monitoring.
*   **Brief Summary:**
    This study investigates the use of hyperspectral imaging combined with a one-dimensional convolutional neural network (1D-CNN) to simultaneously identify microalgae species, estimate their biomass, and map their spatial distribution. Five microalgal species belonging to different pigment groups (cyanobacteria, green algae, cryptophytes, and dinoflagellates) were cultivated, imaged in the 420–850 nm spectral range, and used to generate both monoculture and simulated mixed-culture datasets. The CNN was trained using absorbance spectra and augmented through synthetic spectral mixtures to improve robustness. The selected model achieved high classification performance, reaching approximately 95% accuracy for monocultures and 100% accuracy for pairwise mixtures, while biomass prediction errors remained around 17–24%. Furthermore, the study demonstrated that the trained network could be applied pixel-wise to hyperspectral images, enabling visualization of the spatial distribution of biomass within samples. The authors conclude that the combination of hyperspectral imaging and deep learning constitutes a powerful, non-destructive tool for real-time monitoring of microalgae cultures, contamination detection, and potentially the estimation of other biologically relevant parameters in future applications.

### Non‑invasive monitoring of microalgae cultivations using hyperspectral imager
*   **Authors / Source:** Salli Pääkkönen, Ilkka Pölönen, Anna-Maria Raita-Hakola, Mariana Carneiro, Helena Cardoso, Dinis Mauricio, Alexandre Miguel Cavaco Rodrigues, Pauliina Salmi. Journal of Applied Phycology, 36, 1653–1665 (2024).
*   **Key Topics:** Hyperspectral Imaging (HSI), Non-invasive Monitoring, Biomass Estimation, Vegetation Indices, Convolutional Neural Networks (1D-CNN), Industrial-Scale Cultivation, Machine Learning, Microalgae Growth Monitoring.
*   **Internal PhD Notes:** 
    *   **Source:** Recommended by my PhD supervisors.
    *   **Utility:** Highly relevant for validating hyperspectral imaging as a practical monitoring tool beyond laboratory conditions. Particularly valuable because it compares simple spectral-index approaches with deep learning models and demonstrates their applicability in industrial photobioreactors and raceway ponds.
*   **Brief Summary:**
    This study develops and evaluates a non-invasive monitoring methodology based on hyperspectral imaging for assessing microalgae biomass in both laboratory and industrial cultivation systems. Three green microalgae species (Chlorella vulgaris, Desmodesmus maximus, and Tetradesmus obliquus) were cultivated under controlled laboratory conditions and imaged using a hyperspectral camera operating in the visible and near-infrared range (400–1000 nm). Two modelling strategies were compared: a simple vegetation-index-based linear regression model and a one-dimensional convolutional neural network (1D-CNN). Both approaches successfully predicted biomass concentration, with mean absolute percentage errors ranging from 15–24% for the index-based models and 11–26% for the CNN. While biomass prediction performance was similar, the CNN provided the additional capability of species classification, achieving accuracies between 97% and 99% for monocultures. The methodology was subsequently tested at industrial scale using Nannochloropsis cultures in flat-panel photobioreactors and Arthrospira platensis (Spirulina) in raceway ponds, demonstrating that hyperspectral imaging can reliably capture biomass-related spectral signals under real cultivation conditions. The authors conclude that hyperspectral imaging offers a rapid, non-destructive, and scalable alternative to traditional sampling-based monitoring methods. Although machine learning models provide greater flexibility and classification capabilities, simple spectral indices may be sufficient for routine biomass monitoring, making both approaches promising candidates for future real-time and online monitoring systems in the microalgae industry.

### Microalgae Monitoring in Microscale Photobioreactors via Multivariate Image Analysis

El estudio destaca el potencial de las técnicas de visión artificial y fluorescencia como base para futuros sistemas automatizados de monitorización y control de bioprocesos microalgales.

*   **Breve Resumen:**
    Este trabajo evalúa el uso combinado de imagen hiperespectral y una red neuronal convolucional unidimensional (1D-CNN) para identificar especies de microalgas, estimar su biomasa y representar su distribución espacial de forma simultánea. Los autores cultivaron cinco especies pertenecientes a distintos grupos pigmentarios (cianobacterias, algas verdes, criptófitas y dinoflagelados), obteniendo imágenes espectrales en el rango de 420–850 nm. A partir de estas mediciones se generaron conjuntos de datos de monocultivos y mezclas simuladas, utilizados para entrenar la red neuronal mediante espectros de absorbancia. El modelo seleccionado alcanzó una elevada capacidad de clasificación, con una precisión cercana al 95% en monocultivos y del 100% en mezclas binarias, mientras que los errores de predicción de biomasa se situaron aproximadamente entre el 17% y el 24%. Además, la aplicación del modelo sobre regiones locales de las imágenes permitió construir mapas espaciales de biomasa, demostrando el potencial de esta metodología para monitorizar la distribución de microalgas dentro de una muestra. Los autores concluyen que la combinación de imagen hiperespectral y aprendizaje profundo constituye una herramienta rápida, no destructiva y escalable para la monitorización en tiempo real de cultivos de microalgas, la detección de contaminaciones y la estimación de otros parámetros biológicos de interés en futuras aplicaciones.

### Non‑invasive monitoring of microalgae cultivations using hyperspectral imager
*   **Autores / Fuente:** Salli Pääkkönen, Ilkka Pölönen, Anna-Maria Raita-Hakola, Mariana Carneiro, Helena Cardoso, Dinis Mauricio, Alexandre Miguel Cavaco Rodrigues, Pauliina Salmi. Journal of Applied Phycology, 36, 1653–1665 (2024).
*   **Temas Clave:** Imagen Hiperespectral (HSI), Monitorización No Invasiva, Estimación de Biomasa, Índices de Vegetación, Redes Neuronales Convolucionales (1D-CNN), Cultivos a Escala Industrial, Aprendizaje Automático, Seguimiento del Crecimiento de Microalgas.
*   **Notas Internas del Doctorado:** 
    *   **Fuente:** Recomendado por mis directores de tesis.
    *   **Utilidad:** Muy relevante para justificar la viabilidad práctica de la imagen hiperespectral como herramienta de monitorización más allá del laboratorio. Destaca especialmente por comparar enfoques simples basados en índices espectrales con modelos de aprendizaje profundo y validar ambos en sistemas industriales reales.
*   **Breve Resumen:**
    Este estudio desarrolla y evalúa una metodología de monitorización no invasiva basada en imagen hiperespectral para estimar la biomasa de microalgas tanto en sistemas de cultivo de laboratorio como a escala industrial. Para ello, se cultivaron tres especies de microalgas verdes (Chlorella vulgaris, Desmodesmus maximus y Tetradesmus obliquus) y se adquirieron imágenes hiperespectrales en el rango visible e infrarrojo cercano (400–1000 nm). Los autores compararon dos enfoques de modelado: un modelo de regresión lineal basado en índices espectrales y una red neuronal convolucional unidimensional (1D-CNN). Ambos métodos mostraron una buena capacidad predictiva de la biomasa, con errores absolutos porcentuales medios entre el 15–24% para los modelos basados en índices y entre el 11–26% para la CNN. Aunque la precisión en la estimación de biomasa fue similar, la CNN aportó una funcionalidad adicional al clasificar correctamente las especies en monocultivo con precisiones comprendidas entre el 97% y el 99%. Posteriormente, la metodología fue validada en instalaciones industriales mediante cultivos de Nannochloropsis en fotobiorreactores de panel plano y Arthrospira platensis (Spirulina) en raceways, demostrando que la imagen hiperespectral puede captar señales espectrales relacionadas con la biomasa en condiciones reales de producción. Los autores concluyen que esta tecnología constituye una alternativa rápida, escalable y no destructiva a los métodos convencionales basados en muestreo. Además, señalan que, aunque los modelos de aprendizaje profundo ofrecen ventajas para la clasificación y el análisis avanzado, los índices espectrales simples pueden ser suficientes para tareas rutinarias de monitorización de biomasa, lo que convierte a ambos enfoques en candidatos prometedores para sistemas de monitorización en línea y en tiempo real en la industria de las microalgas.
